Where this comes from

  • After-flood photos: NOAA emergency response aerial imagery, Harvey (Aug 2017) and the Hill Country flood (July 2025). storms.ngs.noaa.gov
  • Before photos: USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program, 2016.
  • Prepared for this event by the organizers: jennifer-fowler/UTSA-flood-images on Hugging Face (1,204 photos and their details).
  • Numbers next to each photo come from the same dataset: rainfall from the nearest weather station, ground elevation from USGS terrain data, roads and paths from OpenStreetMap, high-water marks from USGS, and county income and poverty from the U.S. Census American Community Survey.
  • The AI: Qwen3-VL-8B, a model that reads photos and text, running on the Dell Pro Max GB10 at our table. No photo or answer leaves the building.

What the AI can't tell

  • A normal river, pond or pool can look like a flood from the air.
  • The before and after photos are a year apart, so seasons and cameras differ too.
  • Rain nearby is context, not proof of what's in a photo.
  • Its answers are things to check, not facts. Never drive through water on a road.
